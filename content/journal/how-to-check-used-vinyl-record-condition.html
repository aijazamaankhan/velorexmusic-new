A used record can play beautifully or be a disappointment, and the difference is usually visible before you ever drop the needle. This guide walks through what to look at — the sleeve, the disc, the labels — what the common grading terms mean, and what to ask a seller when you can only see photos. It is written from the point of view of someone buying pre-owned Hindi film and ghazal LPs in India, which is most of what passes through our pre-owned shelf.

Start with the sleeve

The sleeve tells you how the record has been treated. A record kept carefully for years usually has a sleeve to match; one that was stacked, stored in damp or handled roughly shows it on the cardboard first.

  • Ring wear — a faint circle on the front or back where the disc has pressed against the board. Light ring wear is common and mostly cosmetic. Heavy ring wear often means the record was stacked flat under others.
  • Seam splits — the edges where the sleeve is glued. Small splits at the top or bottom are repairable; a fully opened seam lowers the grade.
  • Water damage and mould — wavy board, brown tide marks or a musty smell. In India's monsoon months this matters more than anywhere: damp that got into a sleeve can reach the disc and the inner sleeve too.
  • Writing, stickers, cut corners — names, shop stickers and price tags are part of a record's history, but they are counted against condition, so a good seller mentions them.
  • Gatefolds and 2LP sets — check the spine and the fold. Double albums such as Devdas (2LP, 1st edition) or Baazigar (2LP set) carry more weight, so their spines take more strain.

Then look at the disc, under a light

Take the record out of its inner sleeve by the edges and tilt it under a single bright light — a phone torch works. Rotate it slowly. You are looking for three different things, and it helps to tell them apart:

  • Scuffs — dull, hazy patches on the surface. They are often only in the top layer and may play quietly or not at all.
  • Scratches — lines you can see and, if deep, feel with a fingernail run gently across them. A scratch you can feel will usually be heard as a click once per rotation.
  • Dirt and residue — dust, fingerprints and old grease. These look worse than they are; a proper clean often removes them. Our guide to cleaning records safely covers the method.

Next, check the record is flat. Hold it at eye level and look along the edge while turning it, or lay it on a turntable platter and watch the edge as it spins. A slight dish may play fine; a wave that makes the tonearm rise and fall is a warp, and heat is the usual cause.

Finally, look at the centre labels and the spindle hole. Hairline marks around the hole ("spindle marks") show how often it was played. A label with water staining is worth a closer look at the disc beside it.

What the grading terms mean

Most sellers worldwide use a scale based on the Goldmine grading standard. The exact wording varies from shop to shop, so read each seller's own definitions, but broadly:

  • Mint (M) — perfect, usually still sealed. Rarely used for anything that has been opened.
  • Near Mint (NM or M-) — looks and plays as new, with no visible marks.
  • Very Good Plus (VG+) — light signs of handling, such as faint scuffs, that do not affect play.
  • Very Good (VG) — visible wear, and some surface noise in quiet passages, but plays through.
  • Good (G / G+) — noticeable noise and wear throughout; a copy to listen to, not to collect.
  • Poor / Fair — damaged. Worth it only for a very rare record or the sleeve.

The sleeve and the disc are graded separately, which is why you will see listings such as "VG+/VG" — disc first, sleeve second. When a listing gives only one grade, ask which part it refers to.

Buying from photos: what to ask

Online, you cannot tilt the record under a light yourself, so ask the seller for what you would have checked:

  1. A photo of each side of the disc taken at an angle under a single light, which shows scratches that a flat photo hides.
  2. Photos of both centre labels and of the sleeve's spine and seams.
  3. Whether the record has been play-tested, and whether it plays through without skips.
  4. Whether the inner sleeve is original, and what it is made of.
  5. For editions, what the sleeve or label actually says — our guide to vinyl pressings and editions explains what "1st edition", "180g" and "gatefold" mean on a listing.

A seller who answers these readily is usually a seller who checked the record before listing it.

A new pressing that has been pre-owned

Not every pre-owned record is old. Much of what circulates in India today is a recent reissue that someone bought, played a few times and sold on — such as the Sholay 50th anniversary edition or Saagar on our shelf, both reissues from 2025. For these, condition is mostly about handling rather than age: check for scuffs from careless sleeving and for warps from being left somewhere warm.

Older first pressings of ghazal and film albums, such as Shraddhanjali (2LP, 1st edition) or Saher (1st edition, yellow vinyl), deserve the full sleeve-and-disc check above, because their condition is what separates a collector's copy from a listening copy.

After you buy

Clean the record before its first play, put it in a fresh inner sleeve if the old one is paper and dusty, and store it upright away from heat. Our guide to storing records in India's heat and humidity covers the rest.
